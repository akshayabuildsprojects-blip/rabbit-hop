## 1. Product discovery channels and shopping entry points
- **[Evidence]** Discovery is fragmented across retailers, search and social rather than starting in one place: Sephora (33%) and Amazon (32%) lead overall discovery, with TikTok prominent for younger cohorts and Amazon/in-store/friends-family stronger for older cohorts in a 300-U.S.-consumer survey fielded August 2026 ([The State of Beauty Ecommerce](https://www.wunderkind.co/original-research/state-of-beauty-ecommerce)).
- **[Evidence]** Social often starts but does not finish the journey: in a 511-beauty-consumer survey fielded July 2026, 72% said after finding a product on TikTok their next step is to search for it on Google, while only 7% buy immediately in the app; 48% later buy on Amazon, 10% at Sephora, 9% via TikTok Shop and 8% on brand sites ([Discovered on Social, Decided Everywhere](https://www.stellarising.com/blog/beauty-social-search-discovered-on-social)).
- **[Evidence]** Google remains the single largest first stop (43%), with the remaining 57% split across Instagram (11.9%), YouTube (11.2%), TikTok (10.6%) and AI tools (9.8%); 31% said they use social platforms more for beauty research than two years ago versus 10% using them less ([57% of beauty consumers don’t start with Google Search](https://searchengineland.com/beauty-consumers-google-search-493828)).
- **[Evidence]** The value shopper treats viral products as one option among several to verify elsewhere, consistent with Google owning ingredient/skincare-concern questions (54-66%) while social owns technique/tutorials and is nearly competitive with Google only for dupes/alternatives ([57% of beauty consumers don’t start with Google Search](https://searchengineland.com/beauty-consumers-google-search-493828)). TikTok/Instagram/YouTube examples are qualitative signals of discovery formats, not representative of all consumers.
- **[Assumption]** Observable entry behavior: starts from a need or a price-comparison task (e.g., “blush under $15,” “foundation cost per wear”), opens multiple retailer tabs, and names the specific product/shade to make it searchable in the next step, rather than saving or buying in-feed.

## 2. Influence of social media, creators, trends and social proof
- **[Evidence]** Creator content drives discovery but is separated from validation: nearly half of beauty buyers are more likely to purchase following a creator recommendation in Australian ecommerce research, yet U.S. survey respondents rank influencer recommendations last (2%) for deciding to buy a *new* product, while price (72%) and effectiveness (55%) rank first ([Is Your Brand Really Worth It? Beauty Shoppers Want Proof](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/is-your-brand-really-worth-it-beauty-shoppers-want-proof-new-report-finds-302921803.html)).
- **[Evidence]** In the July 2026 511-person survey, only 9% rate TikTok creators as very trustworthy versus 30% for Google results and 54% for word of mouth; YouTube creators rate higher than TikTok (14.1% vs 8.6% very trustworthy), and YouTube leads for makeup tutorials while TikTok is closest to Google for dupes/alternatives ([57% of beauty consumers don’t start with Google Search](https://searchengineland.com/beauty-consumers-google-search-493828)).
- **[Evidence]** Dupe content normalizes affordable comparison: 28% of consumers in McKinsey’s 2025 beauty survey said they consciously bought dupes and 53% were open to buying them in future, while 50% remained concerned about dupe quality — reported June 2025 ([High-End Beauty Brands Will Need to Work Harder Than Ever to Justify Their Prices](https://fashionista.com/2025/06/beauty-product-price-sensitivity-mckinsey-report-2025)). Social videos framing $5 versions of $25 products as “hacks” illustrate the format qualitatively, not population prevalence ([Dupes and Deals Drive Dollar Store Beauty Growth](https://www.firstinsight.com/press-coverage/dupes-and-deals-drive-dollar-store-beauty-growth)).
- **[Evidence]** Social influence is broad but not the decision: 41% of all respondents made a beauty purchase in the last six months based on something seen on TikTok or YouTube, rising to 60% in their 30s and 34% even in their 50s, indicating the value shopper cannot ignore social proof but verifies it elsewhere ([57% of beauty consumers don’t start with Google Search](https://searchengineland.com/beauty-consumers-google-search-493828)).
- **[Assumption]** Distinction from Trend-Driven: Trend-Driven starts from the trending product and may settle for a dupe; this persona starts from value and treats the viral $38 blush as one candidate alongside $14 alternatives, using creator side-by-sides only to generate a shortlist for cross-checking on reviews, retailer pages and search.

## 3. Product evaluation and comparison habits
- **[Evidence]** Extensive pre-purchase research is now modal: half of global beauty consumers conduct “extensive research” before buying (BoF-McKinsey 2025), and in a 21,279-shopper survey 76% scrutinise products more carefully in the current climate, rising to 84% among Gen Z ([The Economics Behind Beauty's Longer Buying Journey](https://www.contouredcontent.com/blog-post-2.html)).
- **[Evidence]** Comparison depth is measurable in reviews browsed and products viewed: 42% read 6–10 reviews and nearly one-third read 11–25 before purchasing; U.S. shoppers browse an average of 19 makeup products and 12 fragrances before purchase, with reviews/ratings the top global purchase influence relied on by nearly half ([The Economics Behind Beauty's Longer Buying Journey](https://www.contouredcontent.com/blog-post-2.html); [New Health & Beauty research: 5 shopper trends every marketer should know](https://www.criteo.com/blog/5-beauty-shopper-trends-2026/)).
- **[Evidence]** Evaluation spans retailers, not a single site: shoppers who browse across multiple retail sites discover more brands than single-retailer shoppers, and more than half of cosmetics/perfume purchases involve an online touchpoint ([New Health & Beauty research: 5 shopper trends every marketer should know](https://www.criteo.com/blog/5-beauty-shopper-trends-2026/)). In the U.K., about two-thirds regularly compare prices before repurchasing ([Beauty brands navigate the loyalty challenge as discovery channels multiply](https://www.emarketer.com/content/beauty-brands-navigate-loyalty-challenge-discovery-channels-multiply)).
- **[Assumption]** For size/quantity trade-offs (Scenario D), she calculates price-per-ounce or price-per-expected-use from listed size and price when unit pricing is not displayed, and weights durability/longevity evidence from reviews; no direct evidence was found that shoppers routinely perform unit-price math on Sephora/Ulta, so this is a modeling assumption.
- **[Assumption]** Distinction from Practical Sensitive-Skin: both research deeply, but here checks center on functional performance per dollar, wear-time consistency and failure complaints, not ingredient/irritation screening.

## 4. Price sensitivity and perceived value
- **[Evidence]** Price plus effectiveness jointly define value, not lowest price alone: 72% cite price and 55% cite effectiveness as mattering most when deciding on a new product, and subgroups will pay more for noticeable results (56% of GLP-1-experienced respondents) ([Is Your Brand Really Worth It? Beauty Shoppers Want Proof](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/is-your-brand-really-worth-it-beauty-shoppers-want-proof-new-report-finds-302921803.html)).
- **[Evidence]** Premium no longer equals better by default: 64% do not think premium beauty products are higher-performing than mass, with affordable brands raising expectations via virality and efficacy; 24% traded down to cheaper products in the prior 12 months ([High-End Beauty Brands Will Need to Work Harder Than Ever to Justify Their Prices](https://fashionista.com/2025/06/beauty-product-price-sensitivity-mckinsey-report-2025)).
- **[Evidence]** Value scrutiny is the industry’s expected dominant theme: more than half of executives expect increased scrutiny of perceived value for money to shape the next few years, advising premium brands to add demonstrable value rather than raise prices ([High-End Beauty Brands Will Need to Work Harder Than Ever to Justify Their Prices](https://fashionista.com/2025/06/beauty-product-price-sensitivity-mckinsey-report-2025)).
- **[Evidence]** Waiting for a better price is common and high-spending bargain-hunting exists: 67% of millennial/Gen Z consumers will wait until price decreases even risking stockout, and among those spending $2,500+ annually online on beauty, almost 40% were bargain hunters (ESW study of 16,500 cited July 2023) ([Tap into Online Beauty Consumer Loyalty with Deals and Discounts](https://www.accelerationpartners.com/resources/beauty-vertical-discounts-loyalty/)).
- **[Assumption]** Decision rules: lower price vs better quality is resolved by cost-per-use and proof of difference (e.g., Scenario B $50 long-wear vs $22 mixed-wear: prefers $50 only if multiple recent reviews plus wear-time evidence imply fewer repurchases/touch-ups; otherwise defers); a 30%-off product that remains more expensive than comparable alternatives is rejected unless durability evidence closes the gap (Scenario C).

## 5. Brand loyalty versus willingness to experiment
- **[Evidence]** Loyalty is weak relative to retailer/price comparison: only 34% say they rarely switch from preferred brands, and up to 53% of fragrance/skincare/makeup purchases globally come from shoppers buying a brand not purchased in the prior 12 months (commerce data June 1–7, 2026) ([New Health & Beauty research: 5 shopper trends every marketer should know](https://www.criteo.com/blog/5-beauty-shopper-trends-2026/)).
- **[Evidence]** U.S. shoppers trade up and down simultaneously: nearly half purchased luxury in the last 12 months yet most also buy dupes/lower-cost alternatives at least occasionally; price comparison is standard practice ([Beauty brands navigate the loyalty challenge as discovery channels multiply](https://www.emarketer.com/content/beauty-brands-navigate-loyalty-challenge-discovery-channels-multiply)).
- **[Evidence]** Openness to smaller/unfamiliar brands is high when needs/values align: 92% said they are open to trying smaller or niche brands when products align with needs/values in a 237-U.S.-skincare-shopper trust survey (Aug 2025) ([Survey Finds Social Media Least Trusted Source for Skincare Brand Credibility](https://www.einpresswire.com/article_print/838582086/survey-finds-social-media-least-trusted-source-for-skincare-brand-credibility)).
- **[Assumption]** Switching is for value, not novelty (low brand loyalty ≠ high novelty-seeking): familiar highly-reviewed product vs cheaper unfamiliar promising brand (Scenario E) is resolved by requiring corroboration — recent review volume, photos/video, and return-policy/retailer credibility — before switching; without that, she stays despite the price gap. This distinguishes her from Luxury (prestige retention) and Trend-Driven (novelty trial).
- **[Assumption]** No direct evidence was found that Sephora/Ulta site features (e.g., sort-by-price, compare-across-brands tools) cause switching; observable behavior is modeled as using search filters, opening multiple brand pages, and scanning receipt-scannable loyalty options where available, without assuming a specific sale event or coupon.

## 6. Trust in influencers, experts, communities and independent reviews
- **[Evidence]** Sponsored influence is the least trusted signal: only 9.7% view influencer reviews as authentic while 54.4% do not trust them at all, believing most are paid/fake; 80.6% are unsure how much to trust positive reviews generally (237 U.S. skincare shoppers, Aug 2025) ([Survey Finds Social Media Least Trusted Source for Skincare Brand Credibility](https://www.einpresswire.com/article_print/838582086/survey-finds-social-media-least-trusted-source-for-skincare-brand-credibility)).
- **[Evidence]** Skepticism coexists with purchase influence: 76% of social users think influencer recommendations are fake, yet 57% purchased beauty after seeing it on social (89% among TikTok users); 58% would buy because they can see it in action ([Survey Confirms Most Social Media Users Are Under the Influence](https://www.styleseat.com/blog/social-media-influencers/)).
- **[Evidence]** Formal proof outranks hype: lab results were the top credibility driver (56.1% trust as authentic), ahead of influencer/award signals ([Survey Finds Social Media Least Trusted Source for Skincare Brand Credibility](https://www.einpresswire.com/article_print/838582086/survey-finds-social-media-least-trusted-source-for-skincare-brand-credibility)). EMARKETER notes consumers say influencer recommendations do not drive purchases but rank how-to/demonstration videos highly, suggesting relatable micro/nano creators are not mentally coded as “influencers” ([Beauty brands navigate the loyalty challenge as discovery channels multiply](https://www.emarketer.com/content/beauty-brands-navigate-loyalty-challenge-discovery-channels-multiply)).
- **[Assumption]** She discounts single sponsored claims but upgrades creator comparisons that show side-by-side wear, shade matches on similar skin/hair types, and disclosed sponsorship with critical commentary; Reddit threads and YouTube long-form demos are treated as qualitative leads to verify via review volume/recency, not as representative proof.

## 7. Purchase triggers and rejection criteria
- **[Evidence]** Triggers are value-demonstration plus timely incentive: flash sales are the top impulse trigger across generations (60% Gen Z, 59% Millennials, 52% Gen X, 39% Boomers), and email promotions also trigger unplanned buys, but 45% feel overwhelmed by always-on suggestions and many unsubscribe/ignore when fatigued (1,003 U.S. consumers, June 2026) ([The impulse shopper is everyone: Adobe data on 86% unplanned buys](https://ppc.land/the-impulse-shopper-is-everyone-adobe-data-on-86-unplanned-buys/)).
- **[Evidence]** Rejection is driven by unjustified premium and quality risk: 50% are concerned about dupe quality even while open to dupes, implying premium must emphasize verifiable quality/differentiation ([High-End Beauty Brands Will Need to Work Harder Than Ever to Justify Their Prices](https://fashionista.com/2025/06/beauty-product-price-sensitivity-mckinsey-report-2025)). Loyalty rewards (44%), personalized offers (36%) and relevant recommendations (34%) are valued only when they deliver clear monetary or choice benefit ([The State of Beauty Ecommerce](https://www.wunderkind.co/original-research/state-of-beauty-ecommerce)).
- **[Evidence]** Scenario C logic (discounted but still expensive): because waiting for price drops is widespread (67% will wait per ESW) and shoppers compare across retailers before checkout, a headline 30%-off that leaves the item above comparable alternatives is unlikely to trigger purchase without added proof of longer use or lower cost-per-use ([Tap into Online Beauty Consumer Loyalty with Deals and Discounts](https://www.accelerationpartners.com/resources/beauty-vertical-discounts-loyalty/); [New Health & Beauty research: 5 shopper trends every marketer should know](https://www.criteo.com/blog/5-beauty-shopper-trends-2026/)).
- **[Assumption]** Triggers: need-based replenishment, verified dupe parity, or loyalty/points redemption that lowers net cost; rejections: price premium without efficacy/durability evidence, small size with high per-ounce cost masked by low ticket price, unfamiliar brand with thin/recent-only reviews, or promotion requiring bulk purchase beyond expected use. She does not assume every product has a dupe or that similar claims equal similar results.

## 8. Typical shopping decision sequence
- **[Evidence]** The prevalent path is social discovers → Google verifies → marketplace/retailer closes, with channel switching mid-journey: 72% Google after TikTok, 61% switch to Google when social fails, and ~48% close on Amazon; 60% of those who recall first touch switched devices/channels before purchase ([Discovered on Social, Decided Everywhere](https://www.stellarising.com/blog/beauty-social-search-discovered-on-social); [The impulse shopper is everyone: Adobe data on 86% unplanned buys](https://ppc.land/the-impulse-shopper-is-everyone-adobe-data-on-86-unplanned-buys/)).
- **[Evidence]** Sequence length reflects “longer buying journey” economics: discovery Tuesday, week-long alternative comparison, purchase days later after promotional email; conversions stall where unanswered questions remain about value and trust ([The Economics Behind Beauty's Longer Buying Journey](https://www.contouredcontent.com/blog-post-2.html)).
- **[Assumption]** Scenario A ($38 viral blush vs $14 similar-benefit alternative): [Hypothetical] discovery via TikTok → Google product/shade + “dupe/comparison” → read 6–25 reviews filtering for wear/finish on similar skin, watch 1–2 side-by-sides → compare sizes/prices and promotions → buy only if evidence shows parity on valued attributes or price gap is justified; otherwise substitute or defer. Trend-Driven would start from the viral item; value-first treats it as one of several.
- **[Assumption]** Scenarios B/D/E: [Hypothetical] B ($50 proven longevity vs $22 mixed wear) requires longevity math — repurchase frequency, amount per application — and rejects $22 if mixed-wear pattern is consistent; D (larger/higher ticket but lower per-ounce) wins only if usage horizon justifies inventory and expiry risk; E (familiar reviewed vs cheaper unfamiliar) switches only after corroborating review recency/volume and retailer return coverage. Observable actions: sort/filter by price, open 3–5 brand pages, check size/price, scan reviews for “lasted,” “had to reapply,” “broke/expired,” and compare net price after rewards.
- **[Assumption]** Stopping rule: stop comparing when the next 10 minutes of research cannot plausibly change rank-order by more than the expected saving net of time and return hassle (e.g., <$2–$3 equivalent or no new attribute information across last several reviews); then satisfice on best-value verified option.

## 9. Impulse purchasing versus deliberate research
- **[Evidence]** Beauty is the fastest-closing impulse category, challenging any strong “never impulse” claim: 48% complete purchase within minutes/seconds of discovery (highest of tracked categories), and 86% make at least one unplanned online purchase monthly (1,003 U.S., June 2026) ([The impulse shopper is everyone: Adobe data on 86% unplanned buys](https://ppc.land/the-impulse-shopper-is-everyone-adobe-data-on-86-unplanned-buys/)).
- **[Evidence]** Social selling is disproportionately impulse-driven in general commerce, and beauty content accelerates it: 42% made impulse buys while shopping on social; 55% of TikTok users made social-driven impulse buys ([Survey Confirms Most Social Media Users Are Under the Influence](https://www.styleseat.com/blog/social-media-influencers/)).
- **[Evidence]** Deliberation is simultaneously rising: 76% scrutinise more carefully (84% Gen Z), extensive research is reported by half, and shoppers browse many alternatives before checkout, indicating impulse and research coexist by trip type ([The Economics Behind Beauty's Longer Buying Journey](https://www.contouredcontent.com/blog-post-2.html); [New Health & Beauty research: 5 shopper trends every marketer should know](https://www.criteo.com/blog/5-beauty-shopper-trends-2026/)).
- **[Assumption]** Distinction from Time-Constrained Professional: both may limit research, but for opposite reasons — value shopper spends time to save money and will extend research when savings are material; time-constrained spends money to save time. Impulse here is modeled as rare and need-anchored (replenishment, travel size to test value) rather than trend-anchored.
- **[Assumption]** Research-effort vs savings rule: effort scales with ticket size, expected consumption rate and reversibility (high price, daily use, hard to return = deeper dive); low-ticket, easily returned trial sizes get abbreviated checks.

## 10. Factors influencing purchase confidence
- **[Evidence]** Confidence comes from verifiable performance, not prestige: price + effectiveness dominate new-product decisions, lab/claim transparency and review recency/volume build trust, while influencer-only proof does not ([Is Your Brand Really Worth It? Beauty Shoppers Want Proof](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/is-your-brand-really-worth-it-beauty-shoppers-want-proof-new-report-finds-302921803.html); [Survey Finds Social Media Least Trusted Source for Skincare Brand Credibility](https://www.einpresswire.com/article_print/838582086/survey-finds-social-media-least-trusted-source-for-skincare-brand-credibility)).
- **[Evidence]** Detailed evaluative content converts curiosity: brands that make products easier to evaluate (descriptions, authentic reviews, clear benefits) are better positioned as shoppers browse 19 makeup/12 fragrance items pre-purchase ([New Health & Beauty research: 5 shopper trends every marketer should know](https://www.criteo.com/blog/5-beauty-shopper-trends-2026/)). AI explanations that state why a recommendation fits, plus remembered shade/purchase history, also build trust (46%/43% in 300-person Aug 2026 survey) ([The State of Beauty Ecommerce](https://www.wunderkind.co/original-research/state-of-beauty-ecommerce)).
- **[Evidence]** Familiarity plus cross-retailer consistency helps: shoppers discover more brands when browsing multiple sites, and loyalty programs that reward regardless of channel (e.g., receipt-scan models) sustain confidence across the relay race ([New Health & Beauty research: 5 shopper trends every marketer should know](https://www.criteo.com/blog/5-beauty-shopper-trends-2026/); [Beauty brands navigate the loyalty challenge as discovery channels multiply](https://www.emarketer.com/content/beauty-brands-navigate-loyalty-challenge-discovery-channels-multiply)).
- **[Assumption]** Confidence checklist before best-value purchase: (1) consistent performance/durability theme across recent reviews, (2) no clustered failure complaints, (3) size/price math favors expected use, (4) net price after verified promotion/rewards beats alternatives, (5) return/exchange path if unfamiliar brand. Missing (1) or (5) blocks switching even at lower price.

- Methods and populations differ (national censuses, beauty-brand site buyers, category purchasers), dates span 2022–2026, and several findings come via trade-press summaries rather than peer-reviewed work; no peer-reviewed causal study of price-per-use calculation was found, so unit-pricing behavior remains an assumption.
- Social-platform content (TikTok/YouTube/Instagram/Reddit comparisons) is treated only as qualitative signal; prevalence claims from hashtag views or listening counts (e.g., 850k data points, 1.2B views seen in search snippets) were not verified to citable pages and are excluded.
- Specific gaps: No direct evidence found for (a) systematic Sephora/Ulta feature use (sort-by-price, price-per-ounce display, promotion stacking) driving choice; (b) exact research-time-to-savings elasticity; (c) durability/frequency-of-use math routines; (d) Reddit’s incremental role beyond Google/YouTube/reviews. Loyalty-program sale tiers/coupons were not verified to primary retailer pages in opened sources, so they are not named as facts.
- Where evidence blurs archetypes: value, trend, sensitive-skin and time-constrained shoppers all research and all encounter dupes/social proof; distinguishing power comes from *what* is researched (price-per-use/durability vs ingredients/irritation vs time-saving routine fit) and *why* switching occurs (verified savings vs novelty vs safety vs convenience), which surveys rarely separate cleanly.

Less motivated by brand prestige or packaging alone — Supported.** Prestige alone does not imply performance for 63–64% of consumers, and influencer prestige cues rank at 2% for new-product decisions versus price/effectiveness ([High-End Beauty Brands Will Need to Work Harder Than Ever to Justify Their Prices](https://fashionista.com/2025/06/beauty-product-price-sensitivity-mckinsey-report-2025); [Is Your Brand Really Worth It? Beauty Shoppers Want Proof](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/is-your-brand-really-worth-it-beauty-shoppers-want-proof-new-report-finds-302921803.html)).
- **H10. May switch brands when comparable quality at better price — Supported.** Only 34% rarely switch and up to 53% of category purchases come from brands not bought in prior 12 months; most luxury buyers also buy dupes/alternatives ([New Health & Beauty research: 5 shopper trends every marketer should know](https://www.criteo.com/blog/5-beauty-shopper-trends-2026/); [Beauty brands navigate the loyalty challenge as discovery channels multiply](https://www.emarketer.com/content/beauty-brands-navigate-loyalty-challenge-discovery-channels-multiply)).
- **H11. Avoids impulse purchases that do not address practical need — Challenged.** Beauty is the fastest impulse-closing category (48% in minutes/seconds) with 86% making monthly unplanned buys, so absolute avoidance is overstated; at most a relative tendency to suppress non-need impulse versus other archetypes ([The impulse shopper is everyone: Adobe data on 86% unplanned buys](https://ppc.land/the-impulse-shopper-is-everyone-adobe-data-on-86-unplanned-buys/)).
- **H12. Can be skeptical of influencer promotions when sponsored — Supported.** Only 9.7% see influencer reviews as authentic and 54.4% distrust them as paid/fake; 76% call recommendations fake and only 9% rate TikTok creators very trustworthy ([Survey Finds Social Media Least Trusted Source for Skincare Brand Credibility](https://www.einpresswire.com/article_print/838582086/survey-finds-social-media-least-trusted-source-for-skincare-brand-credibility); [Survey Confirms Most Social Media Users Are Under the Influence](https://www.styleseat.com/blog/social-media-influencers/); [Discovered on Social, Decided Everywhere](https://www.stellarising.com/blog/beauty-social-search-discovered-on-social)).
- **H13. Journey: Identify need → Compare → Research alternatives → Purchase best-value — Refined.** Need → multi-retailer compare → Google/review verification → close where value lands fits evidence, but the linear funnel understates loops, device/channel switching and Amazon/retailer closing dynamics ([Discovered on Social, Decided Everywhere](https://www.stellarising.com/blog/beauty-social-search-discovered-on-social); [New Health & Beauty research: 5 shopper trends every marketer should know](https://www.criteo.com/blog/5-beauty-shopper-trends-2026/); [The Economics Behind Beauty's Longer Buying Journey](https://www.contouredcontent.com/blog-post-2.html)).
